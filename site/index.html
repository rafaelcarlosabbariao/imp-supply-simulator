MC² Monte-Carlo clinical supply simulator
Methodology GitHub

Clinical supply · Monte Carlo · R / Shiny

MC² forecasts investigational-drug demand and flags which trial sites will run out, and when.

It models each patient's path through their dosing cycles as a Markov chain, runs that across every enrolled patient many times, and projects IMP inventory at every site and depot under a resupply policy, dating each stockout weeks ahead.

MC² is a public rebuild, on synthetic data, of a clinical-supply simulator built in industry.

The problem

Running out of drug at a site stalls a trial

Patients miss doses and visits slip. Demand is stochastic: patients enroll at random times, move through dosing cycles at their own pace, and sometimes discontinue. Supply works against lot expiry, shipping lead times, and depot limits. MC² models both sides end to end, so a stockout shows up in the projection before it happens at the site.

Context

Where MC² sits in a trial's supply chain

Drug for a trial is made, packed into blinded kits, released lot by lot, held in depots and shipped to sites, where it is dispensed at each patient visit; whatever is left is returned, counted and destroyed. A different team owns each step. MC² is a planning tool for the clinical supply manager, used at the four numbered points below, and the shaded steps are the ones its engine simulates.

1Forecast demand

Before the first patient enrolls, and at each reforecast, the enrollment plan and the protocol's dosing and titration run through the simulation to give units per site, dispensing unit and day. Packaging quantities are ordered against that forecast.

2Seed and resupply

Each cohort at each site is seeded before its first visit, sized off the dosing ladder. Resupply follows an (s, S) rule: reorder when stock falls to s, ship up to S. These are the trigger and resupply levels an IRT system is set up with.

3Watch stock

During the study, a run from the current site and depot stock and the shipments on the road marks every site × dispensing unit OK, AT RISK or STOCKOUT, with the date it first runs out, on the map and the portfolio view.

4Test a disruption

A port strike or a customs hold is a window that delays whatever is due to land inside it. Running one shows which sites it pushes into a stockout, and when.

Outside the model: manufacturing, packaging, lot release, import clearance (only its delay is modelled), randomisation, screening and discontinuation, returns and reconciliation. Depots are drawn down and never restocked from manufacturing (Methodology §4).

Model

How it works

A Markov chain describes one patient's path through dosing cycles; a Monte-Carlo loop runs it across all patients many times to get an expected daily-demand curve, which drives an (s, S) inventory engine with FEFO consumption, lot expiry, and depot resupply.

Features

What it does

Simulate demand

Monte-Carlo enrollment, then a per-patient Markov dosing chain, then units dispensed per site, DU and day.

Project supply

Day-by-day (s,S) policy with FEFO, lot expiry, safety stock, lead times, and depot resupply.

Flag risk

Every site × dispensing unit tagged OK, AT RISK or STOCKOUT, with the first stockout date.

Map and drill down

Interactive world map; select a site for its per-DU detail and inventory-over-time chart.

Portfolio view

KPI rollup across every study and site at once.

Supply-chain news

Live, risk-tagged headlines (strikes, recalls, cold chain) beside the projections.
